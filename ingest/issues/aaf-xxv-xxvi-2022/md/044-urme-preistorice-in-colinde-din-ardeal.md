## Abstract EN
Traian Gherman’s study tackles, for the rst time within Romanian ethnology, the prehistoric layer of local indigenous culture, and of archaic structured carols respectively, built around cynegetic play-scripts: the heroic youth and the ritual hunt of a beast of both biological and mythical ancestry. It is known a fact that in poetic texts with xed form beholding ritual functions (augural, propitiatory, apotropaic, funerary), the geology of successive cultural strata is more visible than in orally transmitted texts with no such xedness. In this sense, Gherman’s previously unpublished study holds a signi cant premonitory value, anticipating regressive ethnological research of Propp-ian kind (devoted to the historical, prehistorical and protohistorical roots of fantastic folktales). The author identi es beyond the fabulous bestiary from Romanian cynegetic winter carols, a number of possible biological patterns which may have held signi cant part within the experiential ground of ritualised poetic aabulatory story-telling: bos primigenius/ auroch, megaloceros giganteus/ antediluvian stag, panthera spelaea/ cave lion, aquila/ vulture. All those patterns are being approached as inherited prehistoric remnant motives mythi cated and transformed by folkloric memory, resulted in fundamental mythical- ctional units underlying initiatory scenarios within pre-Christian archaic winter carols.

               primitiv și boul sur, se face amintire numai de bou sur. Unele amănunte din texte însă ne

               cei cu membre excepțional de mari, de dimensiuni – vorbind în graiul poporului –, uriașe.
               Cu acest colos era nevoit omul primitiv să se lupte tot numai cu arme primitive, e ca să
