## Abstract EN
is study oers both a historical reconstruction and a reective reading of the cooperation between the Folklore Archive in Cluj and the Folk Music Research Group in Budapest from the early 1950s to the present. Based on recently rediscovered administrative records ‒ requests, correspondence, eldwork reports, and acknowledgements of receipt ‒ it retraces a complex history shaped by ideological constraints, personal initiatives, and long-term professional friendships. The narrative follows key episodes: the early student exchanges and joint eldwork campaigns of the 1950s, the political rupture of 1959, the gradual resumption of collaboration in the late 1960s, and the post-1989 partnerships that opened new avenues for archival access, digitization, and joint publications. By combining archival evidence with the author’s perspective as both researcher and institutional heir, the article sheds light on how, even under restrictive political regimes, scholarly dialogue could persist and later ourish. The visual integration of photographs and facsimiles of archival documents serves not only as illustration but as an active component of the historical argument.
