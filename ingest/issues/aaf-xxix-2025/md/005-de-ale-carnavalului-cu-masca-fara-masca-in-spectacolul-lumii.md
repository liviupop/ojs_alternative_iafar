## Abstract EN
The mask originally had well-de ned roles: it concealed, protected and transformed. In the Venice Carnival, it erased social status and freed individuals from constraints. In Romanian traditions, the mask was a weapon against evil spirits, a code of communication between humans and nature. Each mask was created for a speci c purpose: to frighten, protect, satirize, heal, or bring fertility. is is precisely why they were not mass-produced, being unique not only in form but also in function—as part of a spiritual dialogue with the surrounding world. Used in rituals, performances, and celebrations, the mask reects cultural values, beliefs, and aesthetics speci c to each community. Between Venetian and traditional Romanian masks lies a distance not only geographical but also symbolic, aesthetic, and functional. Nevertheless, they remain authentic expressions of the cultural and artistic heritage of the peoples they come from.
